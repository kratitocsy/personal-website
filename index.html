Projects

  • Maglion — wireless EV charging in guardrails

    Charge the car while it drives: solar panels feed coils inside the guardrail, which pass energy across the gap to a coil on the car's side, like wireless phone charging at road scale. If cars charge as they drive, they could carry a battery up to half the size (my estimate from simulations, not a measured result).

    Wireless dynamic charging has already been done by an Israel-based company; the problem is set-up and maintenance cost, because the road is dug up. Mounting coils in existing guardrails aims to cut that by 3× to 5× (a target, not yet proven). Next: a low-cost bench test and a power-electronics partner.

  • Agentidin — a hybrid agentic professional network (in development)

    Hiring for Wynko, I kept struggling to find people with the right skills. On Agentidin, every person has their own agent: bring one (for example from OpenClaw), connect one through AI tools like Cursor, ChatGPT or Claude, or make one on the platform. The agent builds your profile from your GitHub projects, Codeforces and skills you verify, so you're always represented, even when you're offline.

    Companies get an agent too, connected to their company brain and Slack. With a Claude tag or a command, they describe the skills they need, and their agent finds matching people's agents. Agents talk to agents, agents to humans, and humans to humans, without paying for an expensive recruiter seat like LinkedIn's, which early-stage startups and companies in developing economies struggle to afford. The goal: cut hiring time sharply by being autonomous, while staying human-friendly.

    • AI Agents
    • Hiring

  • Kinux — cross-embodiment robot learning (research in progress)

    Moving what one robot learns to a different robot type is a big bottleneck for robotics. My idea: classify robots the way Carl Linnaeus classified living species, grouping them by how much one robot's parts (motors, joints, torque) transfer positively to another.

    Early result: I randomised combinations of parts from multiple robots and matched which parts give the most positive transfer, then stress-tested the combined result. So far this beats transferring a whole robot to another whole robot. These are early results and may change as I test more.
