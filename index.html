Main project

Wireless EV charging built into highway guardrails

Charge the car while it drives, through the guardrail, powered by solar panels along the road. Idea stage: tested by computer simulation, not yet built.

The battery is the biggest cost

A bigger battery means a higher price for every EV.

Charging takes time

Drivers stop and wait to recharge, which limits long trips.

Road charging is costly to build

Coils buried in the road mean digging it up to install, and digging again to repair.

How it works

  1. Solar panelsRoadside power
  2. Guardrail coilInside the barrier
  3. Vehicle coilOn the car's side
  4. BatteryCharges as it drives

The coils pair up and pass energy across the gap: the same idea as wireless phone charging, at road scale. In cities, charging pads sit where vehicles stop.

Why heavy vehicles gain most

Trucks and buses carry huge, heavy batteries. Charging while moving means smaller batteries, less weight, fewer stops and no battery swapping.

Research in progress

Cross-embodiment robot learning

Moving what one robot learns to a different robot type is a big bottleneck in robotics. My idea: classify robots the way Carl Linnaeus classified living species, grouping them by how well their parts (motors, joints, torque) transfer to one another, so a robot can learn from the best-matching donor type.

  • Building an SDK for robot failure logging: when an incident happens, record torque, motor power and similar data from first principles and send it to the cloud for simulation.
  • Early result: matching individual parts across randomised robot combinations has so far beaten transferring a whole robot to another whole robot. These are early results and may change as I test more.
